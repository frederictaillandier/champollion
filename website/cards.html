Cards and reviews

New words become flashcards on the desktop, written by Claude from the screens they were read on. The backend stores them, speaks them, and decides when each comes back.

Making cards

Claude reads the screens with you

Every minute, the daemon takes the words not made into cards yet, about 40 at a time, with the text of the screens they were read on, and asks Claude through the Claude Code CLI in headless mode (claude -p: its own login, no tools, no settings, thinking off). Sonnet takes about 20 seconds per batch and understands the context much better than Haiku. For each word it returns:

  • the sentence it was read in, whole even across lines, with OCR errors and button icons cleaned up (Pomozmna nádvoří → Pomoz na nádvoří)
  • its dictionary form (králem → král), part of speech and gender
  • its English translation, and its meaning in that sentence, slang included
  • the sentence's translation
  • whether to keep it at all: not English UI text, OCR garbage, names or numbers

There is one card per dictionary form. Each time a word is read in the game, it adds a sighting to its card (the form read, the sentence, the translations), so král and králem make one card with two sightings. On the desktop, cards.jsonl holds the cards made, dropped.tsv the words left out and why, and generated.txt and uploaded.txt how far each step got.

Scheduling

When a card comes back

The backend schedules reviews like Anki's SM-2, with two ratings and gaps that start at 10 minutes rather than a day. A success multiplies the gap by the card's ease and 1.3; the ease starts at 2.5 and grows by 0.15 with each success. A failure brings the gap back to 10 minutes and lowers the ease by 0.2, down to 1.3 at least.

A review session50 cards45 due for review, then 5 new, each in random order
First gap, and after a failure10 mingaps then grow by ease × 1.3
Level up when the gap passes7 daysnouns and verbs with a next form

In a session, one card in ten is new, and either kind fills what the other lacks.

The backend

A small HTTP API behind WireGuard

champollion-backend stores the cards in PostgreSQL on taillandier.io and only listens on its WireGuard address, 10.0.0.1:8090. The desktop (10.0.0.50) and the phone (10.0.0.51) are the only peers. The JSON types live in the shared champollion-api crate.

RequestDoes
GET /healthok
POST /cardsAdds cards and sightings; known cards keep their schedule but take the forms sent
GET /cards/due?limit=50A session: cards due, then new ones, with their sightings
GET /cards/due/count{"due": n, "new": n}, which the study lock asks every 30 seconds
GET /cards/{id}/audio?level=1The MP3 of the word, or of its form for a higher level
POST /reviewsRatings; a review sent twice counts once
POST /flagsWrong cards, taken out of the reviews to be fixed

Layers

controllers/ only speak HTTP, services/ hold the rules and transactions, repositories/ run one query per function, and schedule.rs computes the next review without a database.

Pronunciations

A background task asks ElevenLabs to speak every card's dictionary form and forms, the soonest due first, and stores the MP3s. Each word is sent with its language and a period (do.), or a short one gets read as English.

Deploying

The server and the desktop both run Ubuntu 24.04, so a local build runs there. setup-server.sh creates the database and the champollion user, installs the service and opens port 8090 on wg0 only.
